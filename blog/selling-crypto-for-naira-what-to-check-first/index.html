Selling crypto for naira: what to check first

Support Desk · 25 Sep 2026 · 3 min read

    Selling crypto for naira should be simple, but a few minutes of checking before you send anything can save you from the most common problems. Run through this list every time, however experienced you are.

    The short answer

    Before you sell, be clear on four things: what you are selling, the rate, the total naira you will receive, and how and when you will be paid. Only send your crypto once all four are written down in the official chat.

    1. Know exactly what you are selling

    Note the coin and the amount. If it is a coin that exists on several networks, such as USDT, also note the network you will send it on. Sending on the wrong network can mean the crypto is lost for good. Our guide on sending USDT explains networks step by step.

    2. Check the rate

    Look at the sell rate, not only the buy rate, and compare it with the official rate as a reference. Rates move through the day, so confirm the rate just before you trade. Be suspicious of any rate that is far better than everyone else’s. Our post on how crypto rates in naira are set explains what to expect.

    3. Ask for the total you will receive

    Don’t just agree to a rate. Ask: “How much naira will land in my account in total, after any fees?” Get a number, and make sure it is written in the chat.

    4. Confirm how and when you will be paid

    • Your own account. Use a bank account in your own name, and check the account name and number carefully.
    • Timing. Ask how long payment takes, and what happens if it is delayed.
    • The order of steps. Agree what you send, when, and when the payment arrives, before you do anything.

    5. Send your crypto the safe way

    • Send only to the wallet address given to you in the official VersusTrade chat, and copy and paste it.
    • Check the first and last characters of the address and the network again before you confirm.
    • For a large amount, send a small test first.
    • Keep the transaction ID. It is your proof of what you sent.

    6. Check that the money really arrived

    Fake payment alerts and edited screenshots are common. Don’t rely on a screenshot, a message or an SMS alert. Open your bank app and confirm that the money is in your account before you treat the trade as finished.

    7. Keep your records

    Save the chat, the agreed rate, the transaction ID and the bank confirmation. If a question comes up later, you will have everything in one place.

    Red flags

    • Pressure to hurry, or to send your crypto before the steps are agreed.
    • Payment details or wallet addresses sent in a private message instead of the official chat.
    • Someone offering a rate that is much better than the market.
    • Requests for your password, one-time codes or recovery phrase. Nobody genuine needs these.
    • Payment from a third party’s account rather than the platform you are dealing with.
    If anything feels wrong, stop. Don’t send anything until you are sure. Message us in the official chat and a real person will help.

    Quick answers

    How long does it take to get paid?

    It depends on the coin’s network, your bank and how busy things are. Ask for an estimate in chat before you send anything.

    Can I send to a friend’s bank account?

    It is safest to be paid into an account in your own name. If you want payment to go elsewhere, ask in chat first rather than assuming.

    What if the money doesn’t arrive?

    Message us in the official chat straight away with your transaction ID and the details of the trade.
