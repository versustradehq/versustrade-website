Bitcoin vs Ethereum: what’s the difference?

VersusTrade Team · 19 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

    Bitcoin and Ethereum are the two biggest names in crypto, and people often talk about them as if they are the same thing. They aren’t. They were built for different jobs, and knowing the difference makes everything else in crypto easier to follow.

    The short answer

    Bitcoin is digital money. Its main job is to be a secure way to hold and move value without a bank, and many people see it as a kind of digital gold.

    Ethereum is a platform. You can send its coin, ETH, like money, but its bigger job is to run apps and “smart contracts”: small programs that carry out an agreement automatically. Many stablecoins, including versions of USDT, live on it.

    What Bitcoin is

    Bitcoin launched in 2009, created by a person or group who used the name Satoshi Nakamoto. It was designed to do one thing very well: let people send value to each other without a bank in the middle.

    • A fixed supply. There will only ever be 21 million bitcoin. That limit is written into the code, which is why people compare it to gold.
    • Slow, steady, simple. A new block of transactions is added about every 10 minutes. Bitcoin is kept deliberately simple, because simple is harder to break.
    • Mining. Computers around the world compete to confirm blocks, which uses a lot of electricity. This is called proof of work, and it is what secures the network.
    • Halvings. About every four years, the reward for adding a block is cut in half. The most recent halving was in April 2024.

    What Ethereum is

    Ethereum launched in 2015, proposed by Vitalik Buterin and other developers. Where Bitcoin focuses on money, Ethereum was built so that anyone can put programs on the network.

    • Smart contracts. These are programs that run on Ethereum exactly as written. They power lending apps, exchanges, games, digital collectibles and stablecoins.
    • ETH and gas. ETH is Ethereum’s coin. You use it to pay “gas”, the fee for every action on the network.
    • Faster blocks. A new block arrives about every 12 seconds.
    • Proof of stake. In September 2022, Ethereum stopped using mining. Instead, people lock up ETH to help confirm transactions. This cut the network’s energy use by more than 99%.
    • Regular upgrades. Ethereum is updated every few months, often to make it cheaper and faster to use.

    Supply: capped or flexible

    Bitcoin’s 21 million limit never changes, and most of those coins have already been mined. Ethereum has no hard limit. New ETH is created as a reward for people who help run the network, but a part of every fee is permanently destroyed (“burned”), so the total supply can grow slowly or even shrink, depending on how busy the network is.

    Speed and fees

    Bitcoin is slower by design, so for larger payments people usually wait for a few confirmations, which can take 30 to 60 minutes. Ethereum confirms faster, but its fees swing a lot: when many people are using it, gas gets expensive.

    To keep costs down, many people use “layer-2” networks. These are separate networks built on top of Ethereum that bundle lots of transactions together, so each one costs much less.

    Which one is better?

    Neither. They do different things, so the question is really about what you want to do.

    • If you want a simple, long-term digital asset that is widely seen as crypto’s “gold”, people look to Bitcoin.
    • If you want to use apps, hold stablecoins on the Ethereum network, or follow where new crypto products are built, you will use Ethereum.

    Many people hold both. Remember that both can swing sharply in price, in days or even hours. Never put in money you can’t afford to lose, and be careful of anyone promising guaranteed profits.

    You don’t need a whole coin. Both can be split into tiny pieces. One bitcoin is 100 million “satoshis”, so you can start with a very small amount.

    Where stablecoins like USDT fit in

    Stablecoins are crypto that aims to hold a steady price, such as one US dollar. Many of them, including USDT and USDC, can run on Ethereum, and also on other networks such as Tron. Each network is separate, so a coin sent on the wrong one can be lost. We explain this step by step in our guide on how to send USDT to someone without buying crypto first.

    Quick answers

    Is Ethereum a coin or a token?

    ETH is Ethereum’s own coin. Other assets built on Ethereum, such as many stablecoins, are called tokens.

    Which one is safer?

    Both networks are very secure at their core. The bigger risks are price swings, scams, and mistakes like sending to the wrong address or network. Take the same care with either.

    Which should a beginner start with?

    There is no single right answer, and this isn’t financial advice. Many beginners start by learning how to receive, hold and send a small amount before deciding what to buy.

    Do I need to understand the technology to use them?

    No. You don’t need to understand mining or smart contracts to use crypto safely. You do need to understand wallets, networks and how to avoid scams.
